Two-Stage Training Pipeline: Projector Alignment and Joint Diagnostic Fine-tuning
Stage 1 trains the Perceiver Resampler in isolation under a language modelling objective; all other parameters are frozen. Stage 2 loads the Stage 1 projector checkpoint, freezes it, and jointly optimises LoRA adapters on the language model and a dual-branch classification head under a composite diagnostic loss conditioned on FAISS-retrieved clinical evidence.

Fig. 2. Two-stage training pipeline for the proposed vision-language diagnostic system. (Stage 1) The Perceiver Resampler is the sole trainable module; the vision encoder and language model remain frozen with no LoRA adapters. Training minimises the next-token cross-entropy loss ℒlm over radiology report tokens, conditioning the model on 8 compressed visual tokens. The resulting checkpoint (projector_stage1.pt) is saved for Stage 2 initialisation. (Stage 2) The projector is loaded from the Stage 1 checkpoint and frozen. LoRA adapters (r=16, α=32, dropout=0.05) are injected into the q,k,v,o attention projections of the language model, and a dual-branch classification head is introduced. The composite objective ℒS2 = 0.65ℒcls + 0.35ℒlm jointly optimises diagnostic classification and chain-of-thought generation over 7,500 steps (AdamW, lr=2×10−4, gradient accumulation=8, cosine schedule). FAISS-retrieved clinical passages (k=5, selected by ablation over {1,3,5,7,10}) ground both the language model generation and the classification head during Stage 2 training, with 20% of retrieved passages replaced adversarially. Teal: frozen; amber: trained; blue: loaded from Stage 1 and frozen; dashed green: FAISS retrieval flow.
