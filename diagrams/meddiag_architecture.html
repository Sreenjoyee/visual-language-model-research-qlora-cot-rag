System Architecture: Evidence-Grounded Chest Radiograph Classification via Vision-Language Modelling
Spatial representations are extracted by a parameter-frozen convolutional encoder and compressed via cross-attention resampling into compact visual tokens. A QLoRA-fine-tuned language model performs structured chain-of-thought reasoning conditioned jointly on visual tokens and evidence drawn from a dense clinical literature index. A dual-branch perceptron with a learnable temperature scalar produces calibrated diagnostic probabilities.

Fig. 1. Two-stage multimodal architecture for evidence-grounded chest radiograph classification. (I) Posterior-anterior radiographs resampled to 224×224 px. (II) A parameter-frozen EfficientNet-B0 backbone produces a 7×7 spatial feature map (d = 1,280; 49 tokens); encoder weights receive no gradient updates. (III) A two-layer Perceiver Resampler distils 49 spatial tokens into 8 latent queries (d = 3,072); concurrently, a FAISS index over 24,460 clinical passages retrieves the k = 5 most relevant evidence snippets via MiniLM-L6-v2 cosine similarity (k selected by ablation over {1, 3, 5, 7, 10}). (IV) A QLoRA-fine-tuned Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct (4-bit NF4, LoRA r = 16) generates a four-step structured reasoning trace from joint visual–textual context; a dual-branch MLP classification head fuses mean-pooled visual and retrieval representations with learnable temperature calibration and 20% adversarial passage injection (£ = 0.65£cls + 0.35£lm). (V) A binary decision (Normal / Abnormal) is issued at the Youden-J optimal threshold, accompanied by the structured diagnostic reasoning trace. Teal: frozen; amber: trained; dashed green: external knowledge base; indigo: output.
