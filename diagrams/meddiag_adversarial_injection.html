During Stage 2 training, each example is processed in one of two modes. With probability 0.80 all five retrieved passages are genuine clinical evidence. With probability 0.20 the fifth passage is replaced by a clinically plausible but contradictory snippet, forcing the model to rely on image findings over misleading text.

Figure 6. Adversarial passage injection mechanism used during Stage 2 training. For each training example, FAISS retrieves k = 5 evidence passages via MiniLM-L6-v2 nearest-neighbour search. With probability 0.80 (left), all five passages are genuine clinical text and the model is trained to cite and reason from the evidence in the standard manner. With probability 0.20 (right), passage [5] is replaced by a clinically plausible but contradictory snippet whose stated findings conflict with the true diagnostic label. The model must then produce the correct label from the radiograph and the four genuine passages, disregarding the misleading fifth entry. This training signal penalises sycophantic agreement with retrieved text that conflicts with visual evidence, thereby reducing calibration error on out-of-distribution retrieval at test time. The injection probability 0.20 and the restriction to passage [5] were chosen by design: replacing earlier passages would undermine the evidence-grounding objective that drives reasoning quality.
