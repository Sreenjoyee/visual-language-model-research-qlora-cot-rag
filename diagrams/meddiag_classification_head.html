Classification Head: Dual-Branch MLP with Learnable Temperature Calibration
Visual latent tokens and retrieved passage embeddings are encoded through independent normalisation branches, concatenated, and passed through a four-layer MLP. Logits are divided by a learnable temperature scalar trained end-to-end. During Stage 2, 20% of retrieved passages are replaced with adversarial snippets to reduce susceptibility to misleading retrieved evidence.

Fig. 3. Architecture of the dual-branch classification head. (Visual branch) The K = 8 Perceiver output tokens (B × 8 × 3,072) are globally mean-pooled and independently layer-normalised (vis_norm) to yield a single visual summary vector. (Retrieval branch) The k = 5 passage embeddings (B × 5 × 384) are linearly projected to d = 3,072, mean-pooled across passages, and independently layer-normalised (rag_norm). During Stage 2 training, with probability p = 0.20 the final retrieved passage is replaced by an adversarial snippet to prevent misleading retrieved evidence. (Fusion MLP) Both branch outputs are concatenated to form a B × 6,144 fused representation and passed through Linear(6144, 512) → GELU → LayerNorm(512) → Dropout(0.1) → Linear(512, 2). Raw logits are divided by a learnable temperature scalar τ (initialised to 1.0, clamped ≥ 0.1) trained end-to-end to minimise expected calibration error. The binary decision threshold τ* is set to the Youden-J optimum on the held-out validation ROC curve.
